L27: # Before you contribute to a TFSA
L28: ## Updated TFSA contribution room available in CRA account starting April 2026
L29: 
L30: TFSA records from 2025 will be processed by April 2026. This is the best time to check your TFSA contribution room in your CRA account. Always verify your contribution room with your financial institution records to avoid over-contribution. Refer to: 【5†Calculate your TFSA contribution room】.

L37: ## What is contribution room
L38: 
L39: Your contribution room is the maximum amount you can contribute to your tax-free savings account (TFSA). It is specific to you and can change each year, as well as when you make TFSA contributions and withdrawals.
L40: 
L41: If you have more than one TFSA, your contribution room is the total that you can contribute to all your accounts collectively.
L42: ## How contribution room accumulates
L43: 
L44: If you are a resident of Canada, your contribution room starts to accumulate when you turn 18 years of age. This is when the 【10†annual TFSA dollar limit】, which is set by the Government, becomes available to you as contribution room.
L45:

L136: ### New residents
L137: 
L138: If you are a new resident of Canada, you start to accumulate contribution room on the day you have residency if you are 18 years of age or older.
L139: 
L140: Example: Contribution room begins the year you have residency
L141:

L143: David did not know that the dollar limit only applies to him for the years he was a resident of Canada. Because David only became a resident of Canada in 2024, he only has $7,000 of available contribution room (the dollar limit for 2024).

L169: ### Non-residents
L170: 
L171: If you are or become a non-resident who is 18 years of age or older and have a valid social insurance number (SIN), you may also accumulate contribution room if you make withdrawals. However, do not make any contributions until you have residency again. Any contribution you make while you are a non-resident is taxable.
L172: 
L173: For more information: 【11†Do not contribute if you are a non-resident】
L174: ## The TFSA dollar limit adds new room every year
L175: 
L176: New contribution room becomes available every calendar year when the Government of Canada assigns the annual dollar limit. The dollar limit is the same for all TFSA holders and is set every January 1.
L177: 
L178: You may contribute up to the full amount of the dollar limit each year tax-free if you have the available contribution room. You may only contribute more than the annual dollar limit if you have unused contribution room still available from previous years.
L179: TFSA dollar limits
L180: Years  | Dollar limit
L181: --- | ---
L182: 2009 to 2012  | $  5,000
L183: 2013 and 2014  | $  5,500
L184: 2015  | $10,000
L185: 2016 to 2018  | $  5,500
L186: 2019 to 2022  | $  6,000
L187: 2023  | $  6,500
L188: 2024 to 2026  | $  7,000
L189: 
L190: The TFSA dollar limit is indexed to inflation and rounded to the nearest $500.
L191: ### Contributions reduce your available room
L192: 
L193: Any amount you contribute to your TFSA during the year reduces your remaining available contribution room in the same year.
L194: 
L195: If you contribute more than your available contribution room, it is an over-contribution and is taxable at a rate of 1% per month.
L196: 
L197: For more information:
L198: 
L199:   * 【12†How to avoid an over-contribution】
L200:   * 【13†If you over-contribute to a TFSA】
L201: ### Withdrawals are added back the next year
L202: 
L203: Any amount you withdraw from your TFSA during the year will be added back as available contribution room the next calendar year on January 1.
L204: 
L205: For more information: 【14†How withdrawals affect contribution room】
L206: 
L207: ### Fees do not affect your room
L208: 
L209: Any TFSA management fees that you may have to pay, as well as any fees paid by a TFSA trust (such as for investment counsel) do not affect your available contribution room.
L210: ## Types of permitted TFSA investments
L211: 
L212: The types of investments that are permitted in a TFSA are generally the same as those permitted in an RRSP. They include the following:
L213: 
L214:   * Cash
L215:   * Mutual funds
L216:   * Securities listed on a designated stock exchange
L217:   * Guaranteed investment certificates
L218:   * Bonds
L219:   * Certain shares of small business corporations
L220: Investments that are not permitted include any investment that is or becomes prohibited, non-qualified or that has an advantage. Non-permitted TFSA investments are taxable.
L221: 
L222: For more information: 【15†If you owe tax on non-permitted investments】
L223: ### How some investments can affect your TFSA
L224: 
L225: #### Value of investment has no impact
L226: 
L227: Changes in the value of your TFSA investments do not affect your contribution room. Any earnings you make from your TFSA do not decrease your contribution room. Likewise, any losses you have do not increase your contribution room.
L228: 
L229: Example: Investment earnings do not affect contribution room
L230:

L313: #### Watch exchange rates of foreign funds
L314: 
L315: You may contribute foreign funds to a TFSA. However, when your financial institution reports the information to us, they will convert the funds to Canadian dollars using the exchange rate on the date of the transaction. The total amount of your TFSA contribution in Canadian dollars must not be more than your available contribution room.
L316: #### If you have a self-directed TFSA
L317: 
L318: You may move some investments that you already have into your self-directed TFSA, but it is not always recommended.
L319: ##### Do not transfer from your RRSP
L320: 
L321: A transfer from your RRSP counts as income. If you transfer RRSP investments to your TFSA, you are considered to have withdrawn the investment from your RRSP at the 【16†fair market value (FMV)】. This can have serious tax consequences.
L322: 
L323: Because the withdrawal counts as income, you must report it in your income tax and benefit return for that year. You may claim the tax withheld on the withdrawal at line 43700 of your return.
L324: The amount of the contribution will be the FMV of the investment at the time of the contribution. So if you transfer the amount some time after the withdrawal, the FMV may have changed.
L325: 
L326: When an exchange is between two registered accounts (such as an RRSP and a TFSA), you cannot usually exchange securities for cash or for other securities of equal value.
L327: ##### In-kind contributions must be qualified
L328: 
L329: You may make in-kind contributions if they are 【17†qualified investments】.
L330: 
L331: What is an in-kind contribution
L332: 
L333: An in-kind contribution is a seamless transfer of assets (without selling them first) from your regular portfolio (a non-registered account) to your TFSA. Qualified in-kind contributions may include the following:
L334:   * Guaranteed investment certificates
L335:   * Government and corporate bonds
L336:   * Mutual funds
L337:   * Securities listed on a designated stock exchange
L338: 
L339: An in-kind contribution is considered disposed of at 【16†fair market value (FMV)】 at the time of the contribution.
L340: 
L341:   * If the FMV is more than the cost of the property, you must report the capital gain in your income tax and benefit return
L342:   * If the FMV is less than the cost of the property, you cannot claim the capital loss
L343: The total amount of the contribution to your TFSA is equal to the FMV of the property. This contribution will immediately reduce your available contribution room by the same amount.
L344: ## Do not contribute if you are a non-resident
L345: 
L346: If you become a non-resident of Canada for income tax purposes, you cannot contribute to your TFSA tax-free.
L347: 
L348: If you make a non-resident contribution, except for a 【18†qualifying transfer】 or an 【19†exempt contribution】, you will be taxed at a rate of 1% for each month the contribution remains in the account. You may also be liable for other taxes.
L349:
