# Ontario Advisor Investment Simulator

* **Author:** weaponex
* **Date:** 2026-11-21

## Overview

Build an interactive training simulator for Ontario bank investment advisors. The first complete learner journey is a new client opening and funding a TFSA, completing KYC and suitability, comparing a fictional GIC and mutual fund, and completing a simulated purchase. Each scenario unfolds through a client/advisor narrative timeline that progresses in response to the trainee's selections. Product choices and suitability results must use client information entered on earlier screens, so the choices are relevant to that scenario rather than generic. Its first two variations are a client who may need access to the money soon and a client with a long time horizon but low comfort with investment losses. If a trainee selects a product that the scenario's suitability rules flag as unsuitable, an on-screen tooltip should explain the mismatch, cite the relevant source, and suggest suitable available options. The trainee must select an allowed alternative before proceeding to the simulated purchase. Each scenario ends with a recap focused on the rules triggered and citations to relevant regulatory sources and fictional bank procedures, clearly distinguished by source type. The initial TFSA scenario should be able to grow in complexity later through added client facts, decision points, and timeline branches. The design should support later journeys for non-registered, RRSP, RESP, LIRA, and FHSA accounts without redesigning the core simulation, while allowing account-specific steps and rules. It must be clearly separate from live banking activity and must not use real customer data or create real transactions.

## Analysis

The proposed scope covers many connected processes and account types. Implementing all of them at once risks a large and inconsistent rule surface. The first workflow is now bounded to a new TFSA client journey with two initial variations: near-term liquidity needs and a long time horizon with low comfort around losses. Each scenario should have an authored client/advisor timeline whose next event depends on the trainee's choices. The learner should ask follow-up questions and consider the full client information rather than treating either fact as a standalone product-selection rule. When a selection violates the scenario's configured suitability rules, explain which client facts caused the concern, cite the source and section supporting the rule, point to allowed alternatives, and block progress until the trainee selects one. Clearly distinguish legislation or regulation, regulator guidance, and fictional bank procedures, including source links and version or effective-date details where available. These are bounded training rules, not universal suitability judgments. Keep the TFSA scenario extensible through added client facts, decision points, and timeline branches. Add account-specific journeys without a general-purpose rules engine before there is evidence it is needed. State explicitly that the institution, clients, products, and rules are fictional and bounded. Do not imply bank-grade completeness or authoritative investment advice.

Keep product and account rules in one versioned, testable policy model, separate from scenario narration and screen flow. Represent scenarios as fictional client facts, account choices, product details, and expected learning outcomes; keep shared workflow steps reusable while allowing account-specific steps and checks. Each decision should return a structured outcome and reason code. The platform flow and its explanation should use the same decision, so a displayed rationale cannot drift from the rule that permits or blocks an action. Give scenarios and rules versions, and decide whether a learner session keeps the version it started with. Model workflow state and transitions explicitly so steps, eligibility, and outcomes remain reproducible.

The experience needs to control cognitive load. Introduce workflows in stages, show the learner's current task and progress, and offer deeper explanations on demand. Present each scenario as a client/advisor narrative timeline, with events and prompts that advance in response to the trainee's account, KYC, funding, suitability, and product selections. Product availability and suitability results must be calculated from the client facts already entered, including account type, investment goal, time horizon, liquidity needs, and risk information where collected. If the trainee changes relevant client information, recalculate the available choices and suitability result. Each scenario variation should change a small number of clearly identified facts and let the learner compare its result with a baseline; later variations can add more facts, decisions, and timeline branches. When a product choice violates the scenario's suitability rules, show a tooltip that identifies the mismatch, names the client information that triggered it, cites the relevant source and section, and suggests allowed alternatives. Keep the purchase step unavailable until the trainee selects an allowed option. End the scenario with a recap focused on rules and citations, while showing the decisions and client facts that triggered each rule. Provide direct links and section references, and distinguish legislation or regulation, regulator guidance, and fictional bank procedures. Keep the learner's place and entered fictional data when opening an explanation. Expand account and investment acronyms on first use and provide concise contextual definitions.

Keep simulation-only behavior unmistakable throughout the experience. Use fictional seeded data, mock transaction and service operations, visible simulation labels, and safe reset or undo controls. Do not connect the initial simulator to production account, identity, payment, order, CRM, or customer-data systems. This boundary should be enforced in the application environment as well as described in the interface.

Operational needs are not yet specified. Before broad rollout, set expected users, peak concurrent sessions, actions per session, persistence and retention needs, and a cost/load envelope. If explanations are authored and tied to rule identifiers, no model service is needed for eligibility or explanation decisions. If generated text is later considered, it must not determine outcomes and should have strict usage limits and a deterministic fallback. Record enough non-sensitive information to reproduce a session issue: correlation ID, scenario and rule versions, transitions, inputs needed for replay, and rule outcomes. Define redaction and retention limits for logs and analytics.

There is no discoverable existing application code or stack in the supplied workspace, so reuse opportunities and compatibility with existing APIs cannot yet be assessed. Before implementation, inspect the target codebase and decide whether learner progress is persisted. If it is, define a versioned session format, migration or no-migration policy, and safe behavior for active sessions during rollout or rollback. Keep simulation records isolated from any real-client records and production workflows.

The first journey is a TFSA, with RRSP, RESP, LIRA, FHSA, and non-registered scenarios intended as later additions. The FHSA journey should distinguish eligibility to open an FHSA from eligibility to make a qualifying withdrawal, and should teach the applicable contribution-room and account-opening checks. CRA guidance lists GICs and mutual funds among common qualified FHSA investments, but the fictional bank's product shelf and account terms still need to be represented accurately. See [CRA FHSA opening guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/opening-your-fhsas.html), [FHSA investment guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/investments-your-fhsa.html), and [FHSA contribution guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/contributing-your-fhsa.html).

Main unresolved risks are validation and ownership of Ontario-specific rules, how the first journey should cover the two product types, whether sessions persist, and expected usage scale. Resolve these with advisor or training-owner input before turning this brief into an implementation plan.

## Goals vs. Non-Goals

* **In-Scope Goals:** An interactive platform simulation; fictional clients and products; narrative timelines that advance in response to trainee selections; product choices and suitability results linked to client information entered earlier; a complete initial TFSA opening, funding, KYC, suitability, product comparison, and simulated purchase journey; initial scenario variations for near-term liquidity needs and a long horizon with low comfort around losses; tooltip feedback on unsuitable product choices with reasons, citations, and allowed alternatives, requiring a different selection before purchase; an end-of-scenario recap focused on rules and citations; an extensible scenario structure for adding facts, decisions, and timeline branches; a design that can add non-registered, RRSP, RESP, LIRA, and FHSA journeys with account-specific rules and steps.
* **Out-of-Scope Non-Goals:** Real account opening, funding, identity verification, orders, servicing, or customer-data access; personalized investment advice; claiming complete coverage of any bank's live policies; building every account journey in the first release; building a general-purpose rules engine before the first learner journey demonstrates a need for one.

## Alternatives Weighed

* **Static training examples as the primary experience:** The concept explicitly selects an interactive simulation with explainers as its primary experience. Static examples may still help as reference material, but they do not let learners explore how a changed fact affects a workflow.
* **Build all account, product, and servicing combinations in the first release:** Not recommended during ideation because the combination count and rule maintenance burden are high before the core learning journey is validated. Begin with a small, complete journey and a limited number of informative variations.
* **Generate eligibility decisions or authoritative rule explanations with an LLM:** Not recommended because outcomes would become harder to reproduce and validate, while cost and latency would vary. Use explicit rules and authored explanations tied to rule identifiers; generated paraphrases, if ever added, must remain non-authoritative.
* **Connect the simulation to live bank systems:** Excluded from the initial scope because simulated activities must not produce real financial or customer-data side effects.

## Key Decisions & Staging for Next Steps

* Preserve the interactive simulation and contextual explanation as the core experience.
* Establish a hard simulation boundary: fictional seeded data, mock operations, no production integrations, and persistent simulation labeling.
* Use the same versioned rule decisions to control workflow options and explain them; keep rules separate from scenario text and presentation.
* The first complete journey is a new client opening and funding a TFSA, completing KYC and suitability, comparing a fictional GIC and mutual fund, and completing a simulated purchase.
* The first two scenario variations test a client who may need money soon and a client with a long time horizon but low comfort with investment losses; advisors should clarify the client's situation and assess suitability rather than follow a fixed product mapping.
* Scenarios use client/advisor narrative timelines that progress based on the trainee's selections. Product mismatch feedback appears in tooltips, explains the triggering facts and rule, and suggests allowed alternatives; the trainee must select an allowed option before proceeding to purchase.
* Product options and suitability outcomes must be tied to client facts entered earlier in the workflow and recalculated when relevant facts change. End each scenario with a recap of the trainee's choices, the client facts used, and the rules triggered.
* Tooltips and recaps cite relevant legal or regulatory sources and regulator guidance, with direct links and section/version details where available. Clearly distinguish those sources from fictional bank procedures. The TFSA scenario must be extendable with additional client facts, decisions, and narrative branches over time.
* Plan for later non-registered, RRSP, RESP, LIRA, and FHSA scenarios through reusable workflow steps plus account-specific checks; do not force every account into identical rules.
* Identify an advisor or training owner who can validate the fictional rules and the distinction between regulatory requirements and fictional bank product or process rules.
* Decide whether sessions persist, how scenario and rule versions behave during a session, what analytics are needed, and the expected concurrency and retention envelope.
* Inspect the implementation codebase before selecting architecture or claiming reuse. Specify migration, rollout, and rollback behavior if persistent session state is in scope.
